In early January, 2023, I set off for a hike through Morocco's Jbel Saghro with my friends Isabelle and RB.
We were following a [guidebook written by Alan Palmer](https://uk.bookshop.org/books/moroccan-atlas-the-trekking-guide-includes-marrakech-city-guide-50-trail-maps-15-town-plans-places-to-stay-places-to-se/9781905864591), and were turned on to the route after finding a [blog post about it by a hiker named Meghan](https://mountainswithmegan.com/trekking-in-morocco-jebel-sahro-circuit/).
This post is our account of the hike for folks who want to explore the same area in the future.

## The route

We did what Alan called the "Western Saghro Circuit."
This is a __five day, four night__ trek beginning near the small town of Kelaat M'Gouna and ending in Boumalne Dades, each in the Dades Valley to the southeast of the Atlas Mountains.
The circuit brought us southwards[^south] into the Jbel Saghro, northwest through the mountains, and then northwards out of them.
For the most part, _we stuck to Alan's plan, only modifying it slightly on the last day_---as you'll read about later.

[^south]: This is a footnote.

---

The [Jbel Saghro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jbel_Saghro) are small compared with the Atlas to their north, but hiking through them was still majestic.
Our route brought us through three small mountain towns, past dozens of nomadic goatherds, and around countless _azibs_, isolated homesteads perched on mountain sides or off to the side of broad valleys.
The mountains were far less inhabited than the Dades Valley, but in no way devoid of human society.
